Where real time stands

Three steps built, three ahead.

A timed machine is built in the order its parts depend on one another: a kernel that can keep time, a reservation the kernel holds, a measurement of what it kept, a safe state where a motor needs one, a schedule for the microcontroller at the edge, and the robot as a set of machines. This page says where each stands and what judges it. Everything built has run only in an emulator, and nothing has moved a motor.

The six steps

In the order each one needs the one before.

1 · A timed kernelA kernel that can meet a deadline. Built to be interrupted almost anywhere, with timers that resolve to the nanosecond, derived from the fact that a program reserves time and never asked for by name. Judged by the machine reading back, at every boot, what kernel it got: it said 4 milliseconds twice, for two different reasons, before the line said 1 nanosecond.built · emulated
2 · A reservationTime the kernel holds for a program. A period, a deadline, a runtime and a core, on a core set apart for reserved programs, handed to the program before its first instruction. Judged by a boot of two reserved programs and one with none, compared line for line with its kept transcript; by the court's cases; and by four things put wrong on purpose, each refused on the line that names it: the emulator handed one core, the court's bound loosened to what the kernel's setting says, a core claimed and not set apart, a core set apart by half.built · emulated
3 · A measurementHow late the program actually woke. A witness runs the declared period on an absolute timer and says the median, the 99th, the worst and the misses. The kept transcript holds the line's shape and no number, because an emulator's time is its laptop's; the raw transcript keeps the numbers, and six settings were read side by side on one laptop.built · emulated
4 · A safe stateWhat happens when the brain goes quiet. A motor needs an answer in milliseconds, and the machine's own loop runs four times a second, so the safe state belongs to the body, where the loop runs: a declared heartbeat from brain to body, and the body's declared act when the beat stops. On the machine's side the step is small: the court will refuse a health window shorter than the machine can keep, and a reserved program that overruns will be told so, in the transcript.not built
5 · The edge scheduleHard real time, where it is possible. The edge profile, where a program is compiled onto a microcontroller, gains a declared schedule: how often each step runs, in what order, and the worst time its author promises for one step, so that every frame is checked before any chip is flashed. The loop stays the scheduler; the loop gets a schedule. That is the MicroRing substrate's to build, and the floor declares the seat.not built
6 · The robot as a setA brain, a body and the wire between them. A fleet file with two kinds of member and the heartbeat on the wire, judged for the states no single machine can be in: a body with no brain to hear, a brain beating on a wire nobody listens to. A body does not move while its brain is trying an update.not built

One step, shown

The number is the laptop's, and the pin holds none of it.

The witness is a program like any other on the machine. It is given a reservation of 200 microseconds in every 1000, within 500, on a core it shares with a second reserved program that burns all it is given; a third program with no reservation burns the other core. The witness runs a thousand periods and says what it saw. The first line below is from the kept transcript; the second is from one run on one laptop, and only its shape is in the pin.

harb tick, from its pin
# the kept transcript: every number is the letter N
tick: measured -- woke late by N us at the median, N us at the 99th, N us at the worst; the step took N us at the worst; N of 1000 past the deadline
harb tick, one run on one laptop
# one run, on one laptop, in an emulator -- not a claim, and not pinned
tick: measured -- woke late by 413 us at the median, 20421 us at the 99th, 23548 us at the worst; the step took 39 us at the worst; 411 of 1000 past the deadline

Four hundred microseconds late at the median and twenty milliseconds at the worst, with the deadline missed four times in ten: that is what an emulator does, translating every instruction and taking its timers from a host that owes it nothing. Alone on its core the witness did no better. And with its reservation taken away it did better still at the median, because the laptop that runs the emulator decides when its two virtual processors run, and nothing in the machine can promise over the laptop's head. A board runs the same witness and gives the first number that counts.

Said plainly

What the boots do not show.

  • No board, and no number. Every boot on these pages ran in an emulator, whose time is its laptop's. No latency on these pages is a property of the machine, and none is claimed. The first number that counts is a Raspberry Pi's, with a card and a serial cable, which is waiting on hardware.
  • Not a real-time operating system. No priorities, no scheduler to call, no thread to pin. One reserved program per core, one core per program, and what a program starts runs unreserved.
  • Ninety per cent, not ninety-five. The kernel's own setting admits 95 per cent of a core to reservations and keeps 5 of them for the ordinary programs' scheduler; two programs asking 95 together were refused the second at boot, and the court was corrected by the kernel.
  • No kernel's word is taken on trust. Five numbers copied from the kernel's source were wrong when the kernel was asked, and each was caught by a line the machine prints to be read back. The emulator's firmware lists one processor whatever it is given; its emulated clock drifts until the kernel falls back to a tick; a verified clock turns fine only after half a second. The derivation carries all of it now.
  • The body is not here. The safe state, the edge schedule and the robot as a set need a microcontroller, a wire and the MicroRing substrate, which is another part of the same project. The floor declares the seats; it has not filled them.
  • Nothing has moved a motor. The direction is robots and anything that answers a sensor on time; what exists is the floor's part of it, run in an emulator, and the witness that will say how late a board was.
