The model

Local-first by default. One OS under every app.

Two ideas shape how you build on Harobanda — and both keep the operating system at the centre, as the one floor every part of your solution stands on.

Local-first by default

The data lives on the device, and the app works straight from it. The network is optional: when it is there you sync, when it is gone nothing stops. A shop till, a clinic queue, a bank counter cannot pause every time the connection drops — and here they do not have to.

The machine is what makes it real: a fixed network address, and a disk that keeps its data through a power cut, both set up before any program starts — so local-first is a property of the operating system underneath, not a hope inside each app.

One OS hosts the whole Haro family

Harobanda is the floor, and it hosts everything the Haro family produces — each declared as a service on the same machine: the Haro language and engine; the web and agentic server; hy-native mobile apps (hybrid + native) and local-first pages; and IoT and edge programs.

One family, one language, one host. Your rules are written once, and the OS runs them unchanged wherever it places them — the counter, the tablet, the sensor — with no second runtime to stand up and no rewrite at the boundary. A small team keeps one floor, not four stacks.

The connection is a guest, not a foundation — and the operating system is the one host beneath the server, the app, the page and the device.

Named for the Harobanda bridge across the Niger River that joins the two banks of Niamey, the capital. The OS also joins a solution's promises to the machine that keeps them. The patterns on these pages are drawn from the walls of the National Museum in Niamey.
