Blog Data centers at war

Blog · October 2026

A cloud that fits in a room.

In 2026 data centers became targets. Halls in the Gulf were struck and a region's data was lost for good; Russian and Ukrainian halls were hit in turn; the chips that train models were named military objectives. Harobanda's position: a resilient cloud is many small machines in many rooms, each declared in a file and judged by itself, joined by links the file names, so that losing one is losing one.

Read from the news

A hall is a target, and what lives only there dies with it.

The facts below are taken from Cybernetica, Tariq Krim's newsletter of 18 October 2026, “La guerre des data centers, deuxième partie”, which reads the year's strikes on data centers together. They are condensed here, not quoted; the newsletter is the source for each.

One hallDrones struck two cloud halls in the Emirates in March and damaged a third in Bahrain. By September the provider said that what had been hosted only there could not be restored. Clients with a copy elsewhere restarted; the others lost everything they kept in those halls.March to September
The way outThe cables from the Gulf to Europe run through two war zones, so the cloud giants leased a land fibre along Iraq's pipelines to reach Turkey and then Frankfurt. Where a copy could travel became a decision taken in a crisis, by somebody else.March
ComputeThe Gulf had planned gigawatts of AI halls and hundreds of thousands of GPUs. The strikes showed the campuses within range, and the chip maker was named a target. In Russia, two of Yandex's three supercomputers sat in one hall hit in October; the chips are under export control and replacement is counted in months.October
The lawCopying data out of the country in a crisis may break the laws that keep it there. Ukraine had allowed its public data abroad a week before the invasion, and when a Kyiv hall was destroyed its clients were already running on sites in the European Union. Russia has required its citizens' data to stay on its territory since 2015.2015 to 2026
The reachWhen Yandex's halls fell, eighty services stopped and the outage was felt in Kazakhstan and Armenia. A hall serves far beyond its walls, and it falls just as far.October

The position

The unit of a resilient cloud is a box you can carry, not a hall you can target.

Every lesson above is a lesson about size. A hall concentrates data, compute and reach in one place, so one place is enough to lose all three, and the recovery depends on a route, a law and a supplier that are not yours. Harobanda is built the other way round: its unit is a machine that is one text file and ordinary hardware, and a cloud is a set of them.

A declared machine runs from an image built from its file and a kernel fixed by its fingerprint, on a PC or a Raspberry Pi. It judges its own boot against what the file said it would be, keeps a signed record under a key of its own, and tries an update in a second slot before it keeps it. It needs no hall to tell it what it is, and a destroyed box is rebuilt from the file on the next PC in the drawer, judging itself the same machine on its first boot. Rebuilding it is the same act as building it.

A cloud here is a fleet file: which machines, on which links, which box is the way between two links, who dates the records, which key is out. There is no central program that runs it. The smallest one, three machines on two wires with a till reaching a server through a box, is built and boots in an emulator; a nation's or a company's cloud is many such sets in many rooms, a shop's back room, a clinic, a town hall. Losing a room is losing what was in the room, and the file says what that was.

This is where a geostrategic risk becomes a design concern rather than a crisis plan. A concern is first-class when the court can refuse its absence: today the fleet court already refuses a set that contradicts itself, two machines serving one link, a forwarder no route goes through, a time authority nobody can check. The designer writes where each machine sits and what it reaches before any boot, and the solution's intelligence runs on the same small machines, as confined programs with ceilings, outside anything that must happen on time. What a designer can write today, and the five things the file cannot say yet, are on the Security section's page on resilience.

A hall is one decision taken once, by a provider. A room is one line of a file, taken by you, and the court reads it before anything boots.
